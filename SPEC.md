# Pause: an in-the-moment reset for dads

## Problem Statement

When I notice myself getting frustrated with my kids, I have only a moment to change how I respond. I want to hear my child out and find a first line to say back without explaining what happened to an app. When I’m not ready to ask, I need a quick cue to slow down before I answer.

## Solution

Pause is a private iPhone app I can open from a Lock Screen control. It opens to **“How are you feeling?”** If my child is willing to answer, I tap **Ask aloud**. Pause speaks the question, then listens for one answer. When I tap **Done** or listening reaches 30 seconds, Pause stops listening and shows three short lines I can choose from: a reflection, an offer of support, and an invitation to say more. The app does not speak those lines. Launching Pause, including from the Lock Screen, never speaks or turns on the mic.

**Quick reset** is one tap from the check-in. It shows **“Let one breath out before you answer”** and an optional line to say aloud: “Hang on. Give me a second.” Three situation buttons rotate through prewritten cue pairs. If I enable **Speak Opening Cue** in iOS Settings, Pause reads the inward cue when I open Quick reset. Cue speech is off by default.

## User Stories

1. As a dad who notices frustration building, I want to open Pause from the Lock Screen, so that I don’t have to hunt for it before I respond.
2. As a dad using Pause for the first time, I want a clear check-in and a quick reset without setup at launch, so that I can choose how to start.
3. As a dad who has used Quick reset before, I want the same opening cue each time, so that it becomes familiar enough to remember without my phone.
4. As a dad with only a second to look down, I want the inward cue to be the most prominent text, so that I know what to do at a glance.
5. As a dad who needs a second to collect myself, I want an optional line to say aloud, so that I don’t have to improvise.
6. As a dad who only needs one breath, I want to put the phone away at any point, so that I can get back to my kids without dismissing a prompt or finishing an exercise.
7. As a dad who has already asked three times, I want a cue matched to that situation, so that I can hold a limit without getting louder.
8. As a dad who’s running late, I want a cue matched to that situation, so that I can focus on the next thing instead of rushing everyone at once.
9. As a dad who’s being pulled in several directions, I want a cue matched to that situation, so that I can respond to one thing at a time.
10. As a dad who needs a situation-specific cue, I want to choose it with one tap on the same screen, so that I don’t have to navigate a menu or explain what happened.
11. As a dad who comes back to the same situation, I want more than one line available, so that the cue doesn’t become something I tune out.
12. As a dad who locked the phone after a reset, I want to return to the check-in and find a fresh cue in Quick reset, so that I’m not dropped into the last situation.
13. As a dad with no connection, I want all cues to work offline, so that I never wait on a network in the moment.
14. As a dad using this privately, I want no app account, incident history, or usage tracking, so that using Pause doesn’t become a record of difficult moments.
15. As a dad in a noisy room or a quiet public place, I want the reset to work without sound, so that I don’t need to hear audio or draw attention.
16. As a dad who uses larger text, I want to be able to read the cue and reach all three situation buttons, so that the screen still works at my preferred text size.
17. As a dad who uses VoiceOver, I want to find the situation buttons and hear the new cue when I select one, so that I don’t have to search for what changed.
18. As a dad who wants to keep my eyes on my kids, I want to opt in to hearing the opening cue when I tap Quick reset, so that I can take a beat without reading the cue.
19. As a dad whose child is willing to answer, I want Pause to ask how they’re feeling in its own voice, so that the question doesn’t come from me.
20. As a dad listening to my child, I want to see when the phone is picking up speech and decide when the answer is done, so that a pause doesn’t cut them off.
21. As a dad who’s heard my child’s answer, I want three short lines I can choose from, so that I can reflect what they said, offer support, or invite them to say more without having to find the words on the spot.
22. As a dad who taps **Ask aloud** and gets a general suggestion, I want to know what prevented the exchange when Pause can tell, so that I don’t mistake reset cue speech for proof that listening is set up.
23. As a dad whose iPhone is missing the speech-to-text model, I want to download it from Pause when I have a connection, so that the child exchange can work offline later.
24. As a dad setting up listening, I want the download to leave the microphone off and never ask my child a question, so that setup doesn’t start an exchange.
25. As a dad downloading the speech model, I want to see whether it’s downloading, ready, or couldn’t finish, so that I know when to try the exchange again or retry the download.
26. As a dad who wants to stop the exchange, I want to dismiss the suggestion and setup at any time, so that I can get back to check-in or open Quick reset.

## Implementation Decisions

- Ship a native SwiftUI app for iPhone on iOS 26 or later. Use the system’s configurable Lock Screen control to open Pause directly or run a Shortcuts **Open App** action. Document setup and let iOS handle any required authentication; the cue lives inside the app, not on the Lock Screen itself.
- Keep the opening cue and its smaller out-loud line fixed. Pin the three optional situation buttons to the bottom of the screen so they stay in place as the cue changes. If there isn’t enough room for both the cue and pinned buttons, let the whole screen scroll. The reset never requires a choice, a timer, sound, or a completion step.
- A situation tap replaces both lines together on the same screen. Keep the situation buttons available, indicate which situation is active, and bring the new cue into view. Bundle three short cue pairs per situation to start; keep the wording practical and nonjudgmental. Rotate within each situation without an immediate repeat; the initial cue in a cycle need not be fixed.
- Start at the check-in on cold launch and when returning from the background. Quick reset opens on the familiar cue with no situation selected. Rotation can continue in memory while the app process is alive; no persistence is required across launches.
- Put default-off **Speak Opening Cue** and **Speak in Silent Mode** switches in **Settings → Apps → Pause**. The Silent Mode override only applies when cue speech is enabled. Store only these preferences locally; don't save cue selections, playback events, or usage history.
- When enabled, speak only “Let one breath out before you answer” each time Dad opens Quick reset, never on app launch. Don't replay after an inactive-only interruption. Don't speak the out-loud line or situation cues. Stop cue speech when Pause becomes inactive, Dad returns to check-in, or Dad selects a situation.
- Use Apple’s on-device speech synthesis with an available English system voice. Prefer installed Premium voices, then Enhanced voices, then the standard system voice. Prefer US English within each quality tier. Don't require a download or a connection. Use the normal audio route (the speaker is allowed), respect Silent Mode unless explicitly opted out, and mix without pausing or lowering other audio. If speech is unavailable, keep showing the cue without delay.
- Keep bundled cues and situation selection on-device. The child exchange is the only feature that uses a runtime model. No app service, cloud inference, account, or analytics.
- Show **How are you feeling?** on the main screen, with **Ask aloud** and **Quick reset** centered side by side when space allows, and stacked when they don’t fit. Start the exchange only on a deliberate **Ask aloud** tap, after the child is willing to answer. Check microphone permission and local transcription and model availability before starting. Play the fixed question even in Silent Mode, independently of the opening-cue settings, using the same English voice preference as the opening cue. Start listening automatically only after the question finishes successfully. If it can’t play, leave the mic off.
- Transcribe on-device with Apple’s Speech framework. Show **Listening…**, a **Hearing speech** indicator when speech is detected, and **Done** and **Cancel**. Don’t show a transcript or end listening on silence. **Done** or the 30-second cap stops the mic and finalizes the answer. **Cancel** discards the exchange and returns to the check-in without requesting replies.
- After listening ends, pass only the finalized transcript to Apple’s on-device Foundation Models framework. Show three short options Dad can say aloud. Reflect a complete statement from the end of the answer without dropping qualifiers or quoting a retracted statement. Let the model select a supportive line and a gentle follow-up from short, prewritten choices so they don't introduce new claims. Don’t guess feelings or motives, diagnose, or give parenting instructions. If there’s no usable answer, no suitable reply, or a required capability is unavailable (including denied mic access, missing local speech assets, or Apple Intelligence being off or not ready), show **“I’m here. You can tell me when you’re ready.”** labeled **General suggestion**. Never fall back to cloud processing.
- When an exchange can’t start, keep the labeled **General suggestion** and explain the blocker separately when known. Distinguish a missing speech-to-text model, Apple Intelligence being off or not ready, denied microphone access, and VoiceOver suppressing app speech. Don’t direct Dad to microphone settings if Pause hasn’t asked yet. If the cause is unknown, say the exchange couldn’t start without guessing.
- When the required US English speech-to-text assets are downloadable but not installed, offer **Download speech model** alongside the general suggestion. Use Apple’s Speech asset management to install them. Only start a download on that explicit tap, never at launch or automatically during an exchange. Show that a download is in progress, prevent duplicate requests, and show either readiness or a failure with a retry. Keep **Back to check-in** available throughout. Recheck actual asset readiness when Dad next taps **Ask aloud**; installed assets, not an app preference, determine readiness.
- Downloading or finishing a download must not request microphone access, speak, start listening, or resume the exchange. Dad returns to the check-in and taps **Ask aloud** anew. Leaving or locking the app still returns to the check-in; a late download result must not restore the suggestion screen or start an exchange. The asset download does not contain child audio, transcripts, or replies.
- Keep audio, transcripts, and replies in memory only; don’t save, log, or send them off-device. Leave the mic off after one exchange. Offer **Back to check-in** on the reply screen but require no completion step. Leaving or locking the app stops any question or recording, abandons any pending reply, and clears the exchange so the next open starts fresh.
- Support Dynamic Type and VoiceOver using native text and buttons. Use the question as the check-in heading and the inward cue as the Quick reset heading; use the existing accessibility announcement for both updated lines after a situation tap. Skip app-controlled speech while VoiceOver is running so the voices don't compete.

## Testing Decisions

- Exercise check-in and Quick reset through the launched iOS app, using rendered text, button taps, and system background/reopen actions. Don’t expose cue storage or SwiftUI state solely for testing. Check Lock Screen entry manually on a physical iPhone because iOS owns the control and authentication.
- Verify launch shows the question without starting an exchange and that Quick reset shows the opening cue. For situation cues, check that selecting a situation updates both lines, a second tap changes the pair, and reopening the app returns to check-in. Do not pin the random starting phrase or inspect rotation indices.
- Verify the app still works with connectivity disabled. Tap through cues of different lengths and confirm the pinned buttons don't move. At larger Dynamic Type sizes, check that the cue is readable and all buttons remain reachable. With VoiceOver, check that the controls are usable and a new pair is announced after a tap.
- Use the prototype’s existing manual walkthrough as prior art. Use the simulator for screen behavior where practical. On an iPhone, test the actual pocket → wake → control → cue → phone away flow, including locking the phone while a situation cue is showing and reopening from the control. Record whether this takes more effort than is realistic in the moment, not a fabricated latency target.
- On a physical iPhone, verify that cue speech is off by default and the switch survives relaunch. With speech enabled, verify that launches are still silent and only the inward cue plays when entering Quick reset. Check that inactive-only interruptions don't replay it. Check Silent Mode with and without the override, verify the override alone never enables speech, and check airplane mode and playback alongside other audio. Verify that locking, leaving, returning to check-in, or choosing a situation cuts off speech without resuming it, and that VoiceOver reads the screen and announces situation changes without competing app speech.
- In the simulator, verify that a usable answer yields all three reply options, silence or an unsuitable reflection yields the labeled fallback, and leaving during generation discards a late result. On a physical iPhone, check that only **Ask aloud** starts the exchange, the question plays in Silent Mode, and the mic stays off until the question finishes. Test a pause mid-answer, **Done**, the 30-second cap, and **Cancel**. Check that replies aren't spoken and **Back to check-in**, leaving, or locking clears them. Repeat offline with assets installed, and check denied mic access, unavailable local capabilities, and no usable answer.
- Use the app-level UI seam for speech setup. From the check-in, exercise the missing-model fallback, download in progress, ready, failure and retry, and return to check-in. Verify visible text and actions rather than inspecting asset-manager internals. Verify that the setup action is absent when it wouldn’t address the blocker, and that leaving, locking, or returning to check-in during setup doesn’t let a late result reopen the setup screen.
- On a physical iPhone missing the required assets, confirm that a normal launch (including from the Lock Screen) doesn’t begin setup. Verify that the explicit download over Wi-Fi neither requests microphone access nor starts speech or recording. Confirm that an interrupted download can be retried. Once setup is ready and microphone access has been granted, enable airplane mode and confirm a fresh **Ask aloud** tap can ask and listen locally.
- Validate usefulness after a few real uses: did Pause buy a beat before answering, even if the frustration was still there? Keep speech only if hearing the cue helps buy that beat without another glance in the real Lock Screen flow. Ask for a qualitative verdict outside the app rather than building a feedback or tracking feature.

## Out of Scope

- Repairing things after snapping, general parenting advice, guided meditation, timed breathing, or a routine to complete.
- Journaling, reminders, scores, streaks, accounts, syncing, and analytics.
- Custom phrase editing, free-text situation entry, AI-generated reset cues, multi-turn conversation, and sending details about the kids off-device.
- An in-app voice picker, downloads for text-to-speech voices, and recorded audio. The explicit speech-to-text model download is for listening, not for changing the voice.
- Additional Lock Screen integrations or public distribution. Try the app on the owner’s phone first.

## Further Notes

The throwaway prototype already embodies the screen, cue rotation, and background reset. Device-level Lock Screen behavior and in-the-moment usefulness are still unverified. If it takes too long to reach the opening cue, address access before tuning the voice or adding content. If access works but the reset lines grow stale, try a larger prewritten set. The check-in uses on-device AI; Quick reset continues to work without it.
